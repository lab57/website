# Introduction
You see me pretty background on this website, simulating the Lorenz system? In my original implementation, using p5.js, it used an excessive amount of power. I could easily notice how much faster my laptops battery drained when I was working on developing the site. As I started to add longer blog posts, I thought this wouldn't be ideal for readers to lose 10% of their phones battery reading my rubbish, as well as how badly the site ran at all on lower end devices, or if you had other graphic applications open.

I tried a _lot_ of optimizations first. Using WebGL within p5 to use the GPU, precomputing the Lorenz data instead of running it live, and every p5 optimization trick I could find, and it barely made a dent. Finally, I looked into webassembly, and found you can compile OpenFrameworks (a C++ based graphics toolkit) into webassembly, and use it within a web app. There were shockingly few resources online covering this, so I decided to do a short write-up of how I got mine working.
